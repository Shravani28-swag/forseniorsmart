Staying Safe Online

Scams often work by creating urgency or fear. Knowing the patterns is your best protection.

The golden rule

No genuine bank, government office, or company will ever ask for your PIN, OTP, or password over a phone call or message.

Lesson 1

Fake calls and "urgent" problems

A caller claims to be from your bank, saying your account will be blocked unless you act right now.

Hang up and call your bank back yourself, using the number on your card — never the number the caller gives you.

Lesson 2

Suspicious messages and links

A text or WhatsApp message says you've won a prize, or asks you to click a link to "verify" your account.

Don't tap the link. Real prizes and real banks don't ask you to click a link to claim or verify anything.

Lesson 3

People pretending to be family

A message from an unknown number claims to be your son, daughter, or grandchild, urgently asking for money.

Call that family member directly on their known number before sending anything, even if the message sounds convincing.

Lesson 4

Simple security settings worth checking

Set a screen lock PIN, keep your apps updated, and never share OTPs — even with someone who says they're "from tech support."

If you're ever unsure whether something is genuine, ask a trusted family member before doing anything.
